# Main Editor Workspace

## Mode

Operate

## User and job

The primary product user is an AI agent generating 3D assets from JSON recipes. The desktop editor is for people opening those generated `.am3d` projects to inspect, refine, rig, animate, and render them.

## Success

The first view communicates a real spline-based 3D workbench and makes the scene, selected object, editable properties, and animation timeline easy to locate. Existing workspace functions, menus, shortcuts, and file formats remain available.

## Visual direction

User-pinned: Blender meets the early 2000s. Use Blender's compact, task-first multi-area structure with the tactile metal-gray chrome and beveled native controls of early desktop software. Keep the center viewport graphite, object selection amber, and the surrounding editing surfaces cool steel gray.

The direction seed assigned a Miura-fold sheet. Translate its linked, foldable structure into the existing resizable and collapsible work areas. The physical paper, crease diagrams, and unrelated product imagery do not enter the editor.

## First viewport

- Keep the File/Edit/Create/Rig/Workspace/Settings/Help menu row.
- Place Layout/Model/Rig/Animate/Render in the top workspace rail, with a clear active marker.
- Give the current workspace one compact tool row.
- Preserve the large center canvas, stacked Outliner and Properties on the right, and full-width timeline at the bottom.
- Use an actual project scene; selection must be visible in both the viewport and the Outliner.

## Signature interaction and motion

Selecting an object in the Outliner or viewport keeps the selection synchronized and exposes its Properties. Resizable splitters and collapse controls let users fold work areas into the surrounding sheet. State changes remain immediate; no decorative motion is added.

## Product-specific constraints

- Keep all existing editing and file workflows working.
- The GUI must truthfully reflect generated spline projects; never present unsupported modeling or export controls as implemented.
- Preserve legibility at the documented 100%, 150%, and 200% Linux display scales.

## Direction review

Grounded candidates considered: (1) the user's Blender-era 3D workbench, the clearest visual instruction; (2) Animation Master 2005, the product's spline-animation lineage; (3) mechanical drafting, for precise editable geometry; (4) broadcast compositing, for dense tool panes; (5) a Miura-fold engineering sheet, the assigned structure; (6) a film-editing bay, for the timeline and render workflow; (7) a drum machine step sequencer, for keyed animation. The assigned fold system is used for area topology while the user's visual direction governs its materials.

Challenger fusions: labanotation is competitive for the Animate workspace's time/channel organization, but not the whole editor. A drum-machine sequencer is competitive for readable playhead/key states, but its fixed 16-step grammar does not fit the product's keyframes. The mecha command wall is declined; retain its discipline of one consistent state signal as amber focus/selection, without alarms or phosphor effects. The variable-font specimen is declined; retain direct viewport feedback when controls change, without making typography the subject. The gravity-rain garden is declined; retain immediate spatial feedback in the real viewport, without inventing a simulation metaphor.
